Rules right now what each classifier is doing, per stock

About the rules

Each classifier watches a few stocks. Every few minutes it asks Jev (a decision model) whether to act, and only acts when Jev's confidence is above the classifier's threshold. The bars show Jev's last answer, tallest first in the label. control_orb is a deliberately simple benchmark the strategist has to beat.

Jev is only asked once a rule's trigger passes (for example, price above the opening range's high). Today so far says how often each stock was checked, how often Jev was asked, and why it wasn't: the trigger didn't pass (with the values the last time it missed), or the check was skipped (stale market data, Jev paused after errors, the account blocked). Hover it for the raw counts. Zero Jev calls on a quiet day is normal when the trigger never passed.

A probe asks Jev its question on schedule and logs the answer, but never trades. It answers one thing: does Jev's answer predict what the price does next, better than a plain formula on the same numbers? Nothing here counts towards going live.

Results so far scored after each close against what prices did next

How to read this

IC (information coefficient) is the rank correlation between Jev's P(ENTER) and the price move over the next 15, 30 or 60 minutes (cut off at the 3:45 pm flatten). 0 means no information; 0.05 is already useful for a trading signal; anything above 0.2 is suspicious. It's worked out day by day and averaged, and t says how consistent it is across days. Roughly, t above 2 starts to mean something, and t isn't shown at all under 5 days.

Plain formula is a straight-line fit, over earlier days only, of the same numbers Jev was shown. Formula + Jev adds Jev's answer to it. If adding Jev doesn't beat the plain formula, a simple rule would do the same job without Jev. Jev enters: net is the average move after 0.1% round-trip costs when Jev was above the threshold (how many times in brackets); all: net is the same for every answer, the baseline to beat.

How to read the scoreboard
Avg per trade
The mean profit or loss of a closed trade as a % of what it put in, after slippage. The ± is a 95% interval: if it straddles zero, the result can't be told apart from luck.
Race chart
Each rule's running profit or loss after slippage, as a % of the account's starting money, at the end of each trading day. The 1D/7D/1M/1Y buttons count back from the latest trading day, and every line starts the range at zero. Rules take positions of different sizes, so compare the verdicts too, not just the lines.
Promotion
Closed paper trades on the rule's current settings. It needs 20, with positive average after slippage, before it may trade real money. Editing a rule (other than its label, mode or on/off) restarts the count.
Why the intervals are wide
Trades on the same day share that day's market, so ten trades on one day are not ten separate pieces of evidence. The intervals count trading days, not just trades, and no verdict is given before 10 trades over 3 days.
The objective
The mission is to beat SPY on a risk-adjusted basis, so the whole book's return per session (from its unit NAV, so deposits don't count as gains, and including days nothing traded) is the measure that answers it. The per-trade figures elsewhere on this page describe the rules; they don't say whether the book is beating SPY. Exposure is the average share of the account held in positions during the session. The vs SPY interval treats sessions as independent, so read it as a rough guide.
Family totals
A family's figures pool its trades, so a rule that trades often counts for more than one that trades rarely. Check the table to see whether one rule is carrying its family.
One "ahead" rule among many
Each verdict is a 95% test, so with several rules on the board one will now and then look "ahead" by pure chance. Trust a result more when it holds for a family, not just one rule, and keeps holding as trades accumulate.
Leaning the project one way
Wait until a family shows "ahead" with at least a few dozen trades before steering the strategist towards it. Fewer trades than that is a hypothesis, not evidence.

Glossary: day-trading terms used on this dashboard
Day trading
Buying and selling within the same day. Unlike long-term investing, every position here is closed before the market shuts, so overnight news can't hurt it.
Long
Buying first and selling later, hoping the price rises. This system only goes long: no short-selling, no borrowing (margin), no options.
US market hours
9:30–16:00 New York time on weekdays, except US holidays. That's usually 14:30–21:00 UK time (an hour earlier for a few weeks a year when the clocks change on different dates).
Stop-loss / target
Prices set at entry: sell if it falls to the stop (limits the loss) or rises to the target (locks in the gain).
Flatten
Sell every position. Happens automatically 15 minutes before the close, and on STOP.
Slippage
The small cost of trading: you usually buy a little above and sell a little below the quoted price. Backtests subtract 0.05% each way.
Paper vs live
Paper trading uses Alpaca's practice account: real prices, fake money. Live uses your real money.
Backtest (replay)
Running the rules over past days of market data with a pretend account. Useful for testing ideas, but it can flatter a strategy, so only forward paper results count towards going live.
Classifier
One trading rule: which stocks, what time window, what question to ask Jev, how much to buy, where to put the stop and target.
Shadow
A classifier trading on the paper account only, to prove itself before it may use real money.
Kill switch (−5% day)
If an account loses 5% in one day, everything is sold and it stops until the next day.
Halt (−30% from peak, or under $50)
A bigger safety stop. Everything is sold and trading stays off until you clear it by hand.
Growth (NAV) and peak (HWM)
NAV is the value of $1 invested at the start (so it ignores your deposits and withdrawals). HWM ("high-water mark") is the highest NAV reached, and the halt is measured from it.
Go-live gate
The automatic test for switching to real money: at least 10 trading days, at least 20 strategist paper trades, profitable after costs, and no −5% day. When it passes you get a notification and 3 sessions to press HOLD LIVE.
STOP / HOLD LIVE
STOP sells everything now and stops trading for today. HOLD LIVE blocks the switch to real money until you release it.
